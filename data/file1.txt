Project Zephyr - Internal Notes

Project Zephyr is an internal tool built by the DevTools team to automate changelog generation for microservices. It was started in March 2024 by a small team of three engineers: Riya, Tom, and Ade.

The core idea behind Zephyr is to scan commit messages and pull request titles across all repositories in the organization, then use a lightweight classification model to categorize each change as a Feature, Bugfix, Breaking Change, or Chore. It then compiles a weekly digest that gets posted automatically to the #releases Slack channel.

Zephyr's biggest technical challenge was handling inconsistent commit message formats across teams. Some teams used Conventional Commits strictly, while others wrote free-form messages. The team solved this by training a small classifier on a labeled dataset of 2,000 historical commits, achieving roughly 89% categorization accuracy.

Zephyr currently monitors 47 repositories and generates digests every Monday at 9 AM. The tool is written in Go, uses PostgreSQL for storing commit history, and runs as a scheduled job in the company's Kubernetes cluster. There are plans to extend Zephyr to also generate release notes in customer-facing language, translating internal engineering jargon into plain English for the changelog page on the company website.

The team's biggest lesson learned: automating changelog generation only works well if commit hygiene is enforced early. Retrofitting classification onto messy historical data was far harder than expected.
